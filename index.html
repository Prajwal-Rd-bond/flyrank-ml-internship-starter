Content Refresh Prioritization

Applied Search Intelligence — FlyRank ML Internship 2026

Author: Prajwal  |  Lane: Refresh / Content Opportunity Scoring  |  Repository

Abstract. Which content pages in a client's portfolio should a FlyRank editor review first for refresh, given limited weekly review capacity? Using an anonymized starter dataset of 30,000 pages across 32 clients, we built a transparent baseline scoring rule and compared it against three learned classifiers (logistic regression, decision tree, random forest) under a client-holdout validation design. The random forest achieved a ~3× lift in Precision@50 over the hand-written baseline rule (0.74 vs 0.24), meaning 37 of its top 50 candidates carried the observed declining label compared to 12 for the baseline. The output is a ranked review queue with reason codes and suggested actions — decision-support for human editors, not an automated publishing decision.

1. Problem Framing

Decision: which pages a content editor should open and review first this week, out of everything a client owns.

Unit of analysis: one content page (content_id), aggregated over a trailing 90-day window.

The median client has 567 pages. At 50 reviews per week, that's 11+ weeks to review everything once — prioritization order is not optional, it's the whole game. A false positive wastes an editor's time on a page that didn't need attention; a false negative lets a real decliner slip through silently.

Why ML helps: a fixed rule uses only 2–3 signals. A learned model combines 18 numeric and 8 categorical features with interactions a hand-rule can't express, tripling the precision at the top of the queue.

2. Data Safety

Dataset: content_refresh_anonymized.csv — 30,000 rows × 44 columns, 32 pseudonymized clients. All identifiers are scrambled. No client names, domains, URLs, titles, or keywords appear anywhere.

Excluded from features:

  • trend_direction and trend_pct — label source; including either causes 100% leakage
  • Sub-window columns (impressions_last_30d, etc.) — construct the label
  • provider_used, model_used — authoring details
  • content_id, client_id — grouping only, never features
Rate columns are ×100 percentages: ctr = 0.76 means 0.76%, not 76%. avg_position = 0 means missing data (1,205 rows), not position zero.

3. Baseline

The transparent hand-rule baseline score:

baseline_refresh_score =
  0.40 × visibility_score
+ 0.30 × freshness_risk_score
+ 0.25 × position_opportunity_score
+ 0.05 × depth_gap_score

Reason codes: stale_visible_page, declining_with_demand, thin_visible_page, page_one_decay_risk, low_ctr_visible_page, low_engagement_visible_page.

On the client-holdout test set: Precision@50 = 0.240, ROC AUC = 0.627.

4. Model / Analysis

Three classifiers compared: Logistic Regression, Decision Tree (depth 5), and Random Forest (depth 10, 200 trees). All use class_weight="balanced" and random_state=42.

Features

18 numeric: search_volume, competition, cpc, word_count, char_count, log_impressions_90d, log_clicks_90d, log_sessions_90d, log_ai_sessions_90d, days_with_impressions, days_with_sessions, content_age_days, days_since_last_update, ctr, avg_position, engagement_rate, scroll_rate, ai_traffic_pct.

8 categorical (one-hot encoded to 52 total): competition_level, content_type, main_intent, age_tier, freshness_tier, word_count_tier, impression_tier, position_tier.

Target: is_declining_label = (trend_direction == "down") — a proxy label, not a future outcome.

8. Limitations

  1. Proxy label: "down" is defined by a >20% impression drop threshold, not by editorial judgment or future recovery
  2. No causal claims: we cannot say refreshing a flagged page will reverse its decline
  3. Single time window: results from one 90-day snapshot, not longitudinal
  4. Client composition: 32 clients may not represent all FlyRank clients
  5. Metric sensitivity: Precision@50 varies with client-holdout random seed
  6. No text features: the model sees metrics and tiers, not page content
